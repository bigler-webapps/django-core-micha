### Goal and expected outcome

The shared billing gains a contract record per purchase, a durable declaration intake for cancellation and withdrawal (authenticated and anonymous), a full-refund withdrawal policy with separately tracked refunds, and an annual initial term that continues as a monthly subscription through a provider schedule. Each new behaviour is opt-in per app through `billing.yaml`; an app that does not opt in keeps its current behaviour and API responses (new fields may be added, none removed or changed in meaning).

### Settled decisions

1. **Opt-in, recorded per contract.** A new optional top-level `contract:` block in `billing.yaml` switches the policies on for an app. The policy in force when a contract is created is stored on that contract; a later configuration change never rewrites existing contracts.
2. **Continuation.** A subscription plan may name a continuation plan. The continuation plan is a new plan kind that is never purchasable on its own and is not part of the purchasable catalogue. Maana's values (USD 50 / 1,200 tokens for the initial year, then USD 5 / 100 tokens per month) live in Maana's `billing.yaml`, not in dcm.
3. **Transition mechanism.** A Stripe subscription schedule attached to the Checkout-created subscription, created when the first payment is confirmed: phase 1 = the current annual period on the annual price, phase 2 = the continuation price monthly with no proration, `end_behavior: release`. Whether Stripe accepts this under Managed Payments is not yet established (provider docs are silent); it must be proven in Stripe test mode before an app activates annual checkout. dcm therefore also provides the guard below and a per-plan availability switch.
4. **Never a second annual charge.** If the schedule does not exist a configurable number of days before the initial term ends, the guard sets the subscription to end at period end and puts the contract into the review queue. Ending is preferred to an unagreed renewal.
5. **Full refund policy.** Under `withdrawal_refund: full` a valid, timely consumer withdrawal refunds every payment of the withdrawn contract in full (taxes included), minus what was already refunded on each payment, with no deduction for time, tokens, AI cost or fees. `prorated` (the current calculation) stays the default for apps without the block.
6. **Receipt first.** Every declaration is committed with its receipt time before any provider call. Determining validity, stopping future charges and paying the refund are separate, visible states. A provider failure never deletes a declaration or moves its receipt time.
7. **Anonymous declarations never mutate.** A public declaration is stored and acknowledged; it changes a contract only after the account owner confirms it through a signed link sent to the account's own address, or after an operator resolves it. The public response never reveals whether an account or contract exists.
8. **Eligibility is the server's.** Withdrawal deadline: 14 days from the later of contract start and legal-information delivery; with no delivery evidence, 12 months and 14 days from contract start. Recurring invoices never open a new window. EU/EEA billing country remains the consumer-law gate; a business tax ID alone does not make a buyer a business — such a declaration and any doubtful one is stored and goes to review instead of being refused.
9. **Acknowledgement emails** are recorded as sent only after the mail backend accepted them; failures are retried by a management command and never touch the receipt time.

### Risks

- A schedule rejected by Managed Payments would leave the annual plan without a continuation: covered by the guard and the availability switch; activation needs test-mode proof.
- Plan resolution after the transition: the subscription's metadata still names the annual plan unless the phase updates it; granting from metadata would grant 1,200 tokens monthly.
- The public endpoint sends mail to an address the submitter chooses: bounded text, throttles per IP and per address, no account data in the mail.
- Backfill must not change any existing purchase's withdrawal result for current consumers (Maana has no paying customers per the operator; Cinevia uses the shared billing too).

4. Deadline: day 14 boundary; missing delivery evidence gives 12 months + 14 days; late delivery starts at delivery; a renewal invoice opens no window; business tax id alone is not refused but queued; `prorated` apps keep today's results exactly.
5. Robustness: Stripe timeout before and after acceptance (same idempotency key, fixed amount per refund row), process restart between states, double submit, concurrent submit, failed and pending refund never shown as succeeded, provider-originated refund event, out-of-order and duplicate events, a late `invoice.paid` after withdrawal or cancellation does not reactivate or regrant, email failure is retried without changing the receipt time.
6. Transition: checkout completion creates exactly one schedule with the two phases and `proration_behavior: none` on phase 2; the first continuation invoice grants exactly the continuation allowance once and no annual regrant; the next month repeats; plan resolved from the item price even when metadata still names the annual plan; payment failure or required action grants nothing; month-end (31 Jan) and leap-day (29 Feb) anchors; guard ends the subscription when the schedule is missing near term end; cancellation racing the transition.
7. Compatibility: an app without the `contract:` block gets byte-identical responses on the existing endpoints apart from documented added fields, and the new endpoints answer 404.

### Configuration contract (`billing.yaml`)

```yaml
contract:                          # optional; absent = legacy behaviour everywhere
  terms_version: "2026-11-04"      # required string
  seller: "<seller of record, as established by the app>"   # required string, stored on each contract
  withdrawal_refund: full          # full | prorated (default prorated)
  public_declarations: true        # default false; false = public endpoints 404
  transition_guard_days: 7         # default 7, integer 1..60
plans:
  - key: personal_yearly
    kind: subscription
    interval: year
    continuation: personal_continuation   # must name a plan of kind continuation
  - key: personal_continuation
    kind: continuation             # fields like a subscription: name, amount_cents, interval (month), token_allowance, tax_code
  - key: some_plan
    available: false               # optional on any purchasable plan; default true; checkout refuses with unknown_plan
```

### Behaviour map (new `contracts.py`, `declarations.py`)

- **Contract creation** in the checkout-completion branch of `process_stripe_event` for every kind, in the same transaction as `_create_purchase`, idempotent on the purchase. `initial_term_end` = the subscription's current period end (annual) or the package term end. Policy fields from the `contract:` block, or legacy values when absent.
- **Schedule creation** for a subscription plan with `continuation`: `SubscriptionSchedule.create(from_subscription=sid, idempotency_key="dcm-schedule:<app>:<sid>")`, then `SubscriptionSchedule.modify(schedule_id, end_behavior="release", phases=[{phase 1: the existing phase's items/start/end exactly as returned}, {items: [{price: continuation price id, quantity: 1}], proration_behavior: "none", metadata: {app, user_id, plan_key: continuation key}, iterations: 1}])` — check against the Stripe 16 SDK signature; `iterations: 1` + `release` hands the subscription over on the monthly price after one monthly phase so it keeps renewing monthly without the schedule. Run the Stripe calls after the contract row is committed (`transaction.on_commit`) so a Stripe failure never rolls back the purchase; record `schedule_status` `created` or `failed`; `billing_process_declarations` retries `pending|failed`. A failure is logged by exception class only.
- **Transition accounting:** when a paid invoice's line price resolves to the continuation plan, set `phase="continuation"` and grant `continuation_token_allowance` for that period through the existing `_grant_period` reference scheme (idempotent). Never grant the annual allowance for a period that is not the first annual period.
- **Guard:** in `billing_process_declarations`, for annual contracts with `continuation` whose `schedule_status != "created"` and `initial_term_end - now <= transition_guard_days`: `Subscription.modify(sid, cancel_at_period_end=True)`, contract `needs_review=True`, `review_reason="transition_schedule_missing"`. Idempotent.
- **Cancellation (owner or confirmed/resolved declaration):** effective date = the current period end (initial phase: `initial_term_end`; continuation: the subscription's `current_period_end`), or the first period end on or after `requested_end_date` if later. Initial phase with a schedule: `SubscriptionSchedule.modify(schedule, end_behavior="cancel", phases=[phase 1 only])`. Continuation or no schedule: `Subscription.modify(sid, cancel_at_period_end=True)` when the effective date is the current period end; a later date stays `scheduled` on the declaration and is applied by the command once it becomes the current period end. Contract `status="cancellation_scheduled"`, `cancel_effective_at`. Access is unchanged until then (existing `access_state`). Extraordinary: `needs_review`, no provider call until resolved. Team and token packages do not renew: cancellation is accepted and recorded as `effective` at the package end with no provider call.
- **Withdrawal (`full` policy):** declaration committed first (status `received`); eligibility from the contract (decision 8); ineligible or doubtful → `needs_review` with reason, never discarded. Eligible + authorised: in order, each step committed and its state visible — (1) stop future charges (subscription `Subscription.cancel` immediate, tolerate already-cancelled/404; schedule released/cancelled), contract `status="withdrawn"`; (2) revoke remaining grants attributable to the contract (initial and continuation grant references, team pool for team packages), as `complete_withdrawal` does today with expiry rows referencing the declaration; (3) one `Refund` row per contract payment with remaining refundable amount > 0, then `Refund.create(payment_intent=…, amount=row.amount_cents, metadata={app, contract_id, refund_id, dcm_refund: row.idempotency_key}, idempotency_key=row.idempotency_key)`; status from the provider result (`succeeded`, `pending` → `requested`, `failed`, `requires_action`). A timeout leaves the row `pending` for retry with the same key and amount. Refund events (`refund.created|updated|failed`, `charge.refunded`) update rows found by `metadata.dcm_refund` or request idempotency key; validate app, currency, payment intent and amount ≤ row amount as `withdrawal_refund_purchase` does.
- **Public declarations:** `POST /public/declarations/` (anonymous allowed, CSRF protected with the standard cookie; add `GET /public/declarations/csrf/` with `ensure_csrf_cookie` returning `{}`), body `{kind, name, email, contract_hint, cancellation_type, requested_end_date, reason, message}`. Always 201 with `{reference, kind, received_at, submitted: {echo of validated fields}, status: "received"}` — the same shape whether or not anything matched. After commit: acknowledgement email to the submitted address (declaration text, receipt timestamp in UTC and Europe/Zurich, requested end, the statement that the effective date will be confirmed separately); if exactly one active contract of the app belongs to a user whose email equals the submitted email (case-insensitive), a separate email to that account address with a signed single-use confirmation link (token stored hashed, 14-day expiry; link target from setting `DCM_BILLING_DECLARATION_CONFIRM_URL`, a frontend URL template with `{token}`; without the setting the declaration goes to review). Ambiguity (zero or several matches) → `needs_review`. Throttles: 10/hour per IP, 3/hour per submitted email, scope names `billing_declaration` and `billing_declaration_email`.
- **Confirmation:** `POST /public/declarations/confirm/` `{token}` → applies the declaration exactly as an owner action, keeping `received_at`; invalid/expired/used token → 400 `{"code":"invalid_token"}` without further detail. GET of the link page is the app's frontend.
- **Owner endpoints (session, CSRF):** `GET contracts/` (own contracts: reference, kind, plan_key, plan_name, phase, status, started_at, initial_term_end, current period end, next charge `{amount_cents, currency, date, token_allowance}` or null, cancel_effective_at, withdrawal `{eligible, reason, deadline}`, declarations `[{reference, kind, status, received_at, effective_at}]`, refunds `[{status, amount_cents, currency, due_by}]`); `POST contracts/<reference>/cancel/` `{cancellation_type, requested_end_date, reason}` → declaration (channel account) applied immediately when ordinary; `POST contracts/<reference>/withdraw/` `{confirm: true}`. Another user's reference → 404. Team members who are not the purchaser see no contract and get 404.
- **Existing withdrawal endpoints:** keep paths and shapes. For a `full`-policy purchase: list rows carry `deadline` (ISO, server-computed) and `policy: "full"`; preview returns `{policy: "full", eligible, reason, deadline, amount_paid_cents, refunded_cents, refund_cents, currency}` and none of the time/token share fields; POST runs the declaration path and returns `{status, declaration_reference, refund_status, refund_cents, withdrawn_at}`. Legacy purchases keep today's response.
- **`me/`:** add `contract` (the chosen subscription's contract summary as in `contracts/`, or null) — additive only.
- **Operator (`_guard`):** `GET operator/declarations/?status=&overdue=1` (paginated, minimal fields, no Stripe ids), `GET operator/overview/` gains `declarations_needing_review` and `refunds_overdue` counts (additive).
